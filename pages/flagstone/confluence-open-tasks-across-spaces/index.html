How tasks work in Confluence

A task is a checkbox on a page (an action item). Mention a person in it and it is assigned to them; add a date and it has a due date. It stays open until someone ticks it on that page. Nothing else happens: a task that is three weeks late looks the same as one created this morning, unless somebody opens the page.

So the task itself is easy. Finding it again is the hard part, and Confluence gives you two tools for that.

1. Your own task list

  • Where: open your profile menu (your picture, top right) and choose Tasks.
  • What it shows: the tasks assigned to you and the tasks you created, open or completed, from the pages you can see.
  • Good for: your own work. It does not answer a lead's questions: what is open in my team, who is overloaded, which tasks belong to nobody.

2. The Task report macro

  • Where: edit a page, type /task report and insert the macro.
  • What it shows: a list of tasks from the spaces and pages you choose in its settings, narrowed by who has them and whether they are complete.
  • Good for: a team page or a project home page that lists that team's or that project's open tasks.

A report is only as wide as the spaces and pages it was set up with. A new space is not in it until someone edits the macro.

Where it stops

The Task report macro does not pull tasks from every space of a site. Atlassian tracks the request to make it do so in its public ticket CONFCLOUD-71390. Until then, three questions stay hard to answer:

QuestionBy hand
What is open across all our spaces?One report per group of spaces, kept up to date as spaces are added
What is overdue, and whose is it?Read each report and compare the dates yourself
Which tasks have no assignee?Easy to miss: nobody's task list shows them

Habits that help

  • Always mention a person. A task without an assignee appears in nobody's list.
  • Always add a date. Without one, a task can never be late, so it never gets attention.
  • One report per team page. Put a Task report on each team's home page and check its spaces when a new space is created.
  • Close the loop in the next meeting. Start from the last meeting's notes and tick or reassign what is there.

One list for every space with Flagstone

Flagstone is a Confluence Cloud app that gathers the open tasks from every space you can see. My work groups your tasks into Overdue, Due today, Next 7 days, Later and No due date, for tasks assigned to you or created by you. All tasks lists every open task you can see, overdue first, with a space filter. Team counts open, overdue and due-this-week tasks per person, with a "Nobody assigned" row for the tasks without an assignee. A badge under each page's title counts that page's open tasks, and the Flagstone report macro puts a live list on a team page or in meeting notes, for all spaces or the ones you pick.

  • Read-only: built on Atlassian Forge with read scopes only. It cannot tick a task or resolve a comment; every row links to Confluence, where you do that as usual.
  • Your permissions: everything is read live as the person looking, so nobody sees a task or comment on a page Confluence would hide from them.
  • Nothing leaves Atlassian: no outside servers ("Runs on Atlassian"). For the same reason it sends no email or Slack reminders. It stores counts per space only, never task or comment text, page titles or people.
