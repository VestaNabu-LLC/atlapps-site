Every unresolved inline comment

An inline comment is a question pinned to a sentence: "is this still accurate?", "who decides this?". It stays open until someone resolves it, on that page only. Across a site, open comments pile up where nobody looks. Here is what Confluence Cloud shows by itself, where it stops, and how to list them all.

How inline comments work

Select text on a page and choose to comment: the text is highlighted and the comment opens beside it. People reply in the same thread. When the question is answered, someone clicks Resolve; the highlight goes away and the thread is kept with the page's resolved comments. A resolved comment can be reopened.

Until then the comment is unresolved: an open question about a specific sentence.

What you can see on one page

  • Open comments: the highlights on the page. Click one to read the thread.
  • Resolved comments: in the page's menu (the three dots), under Resolved comments.
  • Notifications: the page's author and watchers hear about a new comment once, when it is written. Nothing reminds anyone that it is still open a month later.

Where it stops

All of that is per page. Confluence has no view of all unresolved inline comments across pages or spaces. Atlassian tracks the request in its public ticket CONFCLOUD-34510. So these questions have no built-in answer:

QuestionBy hand
Which comments on my pages are still open?Open each page you wrote and look for highlights
Which comments has nobody answered for a month?Open each thread and read its date
How many open comments does a space have?Page by page

Why open comments matter

An unresolved comment on a policy, a runbook or a specification means a reader doubted the text and nobody confirmed or corrected it. The next reader sees the same sentence without the doubt, or sees the highlight and stops trusting the page. Either way the page is worth less than it looks. Old open comments are also the cheapest review you will ever get: somebody already found the weak spot for you.

Habits that help

  • The page's author resolves. Agree that whoever owns the page answers and resolves each comment, so every comment has an owner.
  • Resolve when you answer. A reply without Resolve leaves the comment open forever.
  • Review before you publish again. When you update a page, walk through its highlights first.
  • Sweep the important spaces. Once a quarter, open the pages people rely on most and clear what is open.
